Helper's Guide

This page is for the person helping — written in plain words, in large print. You can and keep it next to the reading desk.

Is everything working?

Press this one button. It checks the whole reader and tells you in plain words if anything needs attention.

What this app does

Blind Reader uses the camera to look at a page, a letter, a pill bottle — anything — and reads it out loud in a natural voice. It is made so a blind person can use it alone. It works the same on a phone or tablet (by touch) and on a computer with a webcam (by keyboard). Your job as helper is only for the rare moments something goes wrong.

Good to know: the reading itself is done by an AI service on the internet that was connected during setup. The "key" in Setup is like a library card from the AI company — the app shows it whenever it asks for a reading. Some of these services are paid — a few cents per page — and one, Google Gemini, has a free plan. That is also why the reader needs an internet connection to work.

How he uses it on the phone

  • Tap anywhere on the top of the screen — takes a picture and reads it aloud.
  • Tap again while it is talking — the voice stops.
  • Three wide buttons along the bottom, left to right:
    • Again (left) — continues where the voice stopped, or repeats.
    • Ask (middle) — he speaks a question and hears the answer.
    • Help (right) — the app explains itself out loud.
  • Slide a finger up or down the screen — voice talks faster or slower.
  • Hold a finger down anywhere — spoken help.

How he uses it on the computer

KeyWhat it does
Space barTake a picture and read it — or stop the voice

If he presses any other key, the app tells him the controls out loud. The book lies flat on the desk with the camera above it: turn page, press space, listen.

The golden rule

When in doubt: close the app completely and open it again. If that doesn't help, turn the phone (or computer) off and on. This fixes almost everything.

To close an app completely on an iPhone: swipe up from the very bottom of the screen and hold your finger for a second — you'll see the open apps — then push the reader's picture up and off the screen. On Android: tap the square button (or swipe up and hold), then swipe the reader away. Then open it again from its icon.

When something goes wrong

What you see or hearWhat to do
The voice says "the access key was rejected" Tap Setup twice (top right corner of the reader). Check the API key is filled in, press Test the connection, then Save.
The voice says "could not reach the internet" The Wi-Fi or phone signal is off or down. Check Wi-Fi is on. Try opening any website.
No sound at all — the app seems to work but stays silent Turn the volume up with the side buttons. On iPhone, also check the small switch on the left side of the phone — if it shows orange, the phone is on silent and the voice may be muted. Flip it back.
The voice says "the free reading allowance is used up" The free service has a daily limit. It resets by itself — try again in a few hours or tomorrow. (Or the Setup page can switch to a paid service.)
The picture is black or it says the camera won't open Make sure nothing covers the camera on the back. If it asked for camera permission and someone pressed "Don't allow": open the phone's Settings, find the browser or Blind Reader, and allow the Camera.
It talks much too fast or too slow Slide one finger up (faster) or down (slower) on the reading screen. Each slide changes it one step and the app says so.
He pressed something and now the screen looks different / he's "somewhere else" The golden rule: close the app completely and open it again. It always comes back to the same simple reading screen.
It keeps misunderstanding his spoken questions Quiet the room (TV off), hold the phone a little closer when he speaks, and have him press Ask, wait for "What is your question?", then speak.
The reading itself seems wrong or garbled Usually the photo was poor. Turn on the desk lamp — poor light is the number one cause. Hold the phone about a foot above the page, keep pages flat, hold still for a second after tapping. The app itself will also coach him out loud.

Setting up a new phone or computer

The reader's settings live inside each device, so a new phone or computer starts empty. The easy way: on a device that already works, open Setup (tap the word Setup twice, top right), scroll to "Set up another phone without typing", press Create setup link, and send that link to the new device by text message or email. Open the link there — done. It will say "Setup complete."

On a computer, use Chrome or Edge (voice questions don't work in Firefox), and pick the right camera in Setup if the computer has more than one.

Nothing here can be broken

Don't be afraid to press things. The reader has no delete, no buy, no send — every button only ever talks. The worst that can happen is it reads something twice.
